Task 1: Importance of Four Fundamental Forces

1. Gravitational Force

Importance: The weakest yet infinitely ranging force that attracts masses together, maintaining the structure of the universe, planetary orbits, and keeping us grounded on Earth. Real-life example: An apple falling from a tree to the ground.

2. Electromagnetic Force

Importance: Controls interactions between electrically charged particles, forming atoms (electrons orbiting nuclei), molecular bonds, light, and all electrical technology. Real-life example: A magnet attracting iron filings.

3. Strong Nuclear Force

Importance: The strongest force in nature operating at the subatomic level, overcoming electrostatic repulsion between protons to bind atomic nuclei stably together. Real-life example: Energy production in the sun and stars via nuclear fusion.

4. Weak Nuclear Force

Importance: Responsible for radioactive decay and changing subatomic particle types, playing a key role in nuclear reactions inside the sun. Real-life example: Carbon dating used to determine the age of ancient bones and artifacts.
